## 2. The central problem

We have supply (musicians) and very little demand (churches posting needs). If musicians sign up and see zero opportunities for a month, they leave. If a church signs up and sees no available matches for their instrument, they leave.

**The critical path is church adoption.** Every church that joins and posts real needs creates paying opportunities for musicians. Every completed transaction proves the model and generates testimonials. The network effect only starts working once both sides are active.

## 3. The core insight: We are selling a cultural change, not a tool

Churches that already budget for guest musicians are easy wins but rare. The harder and more valuable win is churches that haven't thought about paying musicians — because they haven't had a way to find them, or they assume volunteer-only is the only model.

Our pitch is not "here's an app to hire musicians." It's:

> Paying a guest musician is not a luxury. It is a way of saying: *your time, preparation, and skill matter to our church.* Sunday Musician makes it simple to find, book, and compensate reliable musicians for your worship team.

The $5 fee is small enough to be frictionless ($5 on a $100-150 musician rate = 3-5%). Position it as "covers payment processing and matching" — comparable to what every other service platform charges, and lower than most.

### What this means for positioning

- **To churches:** "You already need guest musicians sometimes. Now there is a simple, dignified way to get them — and pay them fairly."
- **To musicians:** "You will be compensated for your preparation and skill. This is not a volunteer matching board."
- **To the market:** Sunday Musician exists not just to fill slots but to raise the standard of how churches treat musicians.

## 5. Phase 2: What to do about musicians (Ongoing, lightweight)

**The rule:** Do not actively recruit more musicians until 5-8 churches are posting needs. New musicians who join and see zero opportunities will churn faster than they signed up.

### What to do instead:

**Retain existing musicians:**
- Monthly email: "Here are the current needs posted by Portland-area churches."
- If there are zero needs that month: "We're actively recruiting churches. Here's what we're doing this week. If you know a church that needs reliable musicians, send them our way."
- Each new church posting gets matched to relevant musicians immediately. Push notifications are critical here.

**Fill specific gaps only:**
- When a church posts a need and no musicians in their area play that instrument, post a targeted musician call: "We have a Beaverton church looking for a drummer. Know anyone?"
- Use personal network + the existing musician list for referrals.

### When to expand musician recruitment:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| 5+ churches posting regularly | Open musician invites to public social posts |
| 2+ repeat postings from same church | Ask church to share Sunday Musician with their musician network |
| 15+ available musicians, 3+ instruments underrepresented | Targeted invite to fill that gap |

## 7. Payment model positioning

Because we are the billing layer and take $5 per transaction:

| Musician rate | Church pays | Our fee % |
|---|---|---|
| $50 | $55 | 10% |
| $75 | $80 | 6.7% |
| $100 | $105 | 5% |
| $150 | $155 | 3.3% |

**Key talking points for churches:**
- "The fee covers payment processing, so you don't have to deal with cash, checks, or awkward conversations about payment."
- "Musicians set their own rate. You approve it before booking. No surprises."
- "Free to post. Free to browse. You only pay when you book."

**Key talking points for musicians:**
- "You set your rate. You keep 100% of what you charge. The church pays a $5 service fee on top."
- "Payment is handled through the platform. No waiting, no awkward follow-ups."

## 8. The 90-day milestone framework

| Metric | 30 days | 60 days | 90 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active churches (posted at least 1 need) | 3-5 | 8-12 | 15-20 |
| Total needs posted | 5-8 | 15-20 | 30-40 |
| Active musicians with at least 1 offer | Current | Current + 5 | Current + 10 |
| Churches with repeat postings | 0 | 2-3 | 6-8 |
| Completed transactions through platform | 2-3 | 8-12 | 20+ |
| Gross payment volume through platform | $200-400 | $800-1,500 | $2,000-4,000 |

**Why these numbers:**
- 15-20 active churches means a church posting on any given weekend will get matched. That's the minimum viable network effect.
- 20+ completed transactions generates testimonials, social proof, and repeat behavior.
- $2,000-4,000 in payment volume = $100-200 in platform revenue. Revenue is not the goal at this stage — it's a validation metric. If churches are willing to pay through the platform, the model works.

## 9. Green flags, yellow flags, and red flags

### Green flags (on track):
- 5+ churches posting needs within 6 weeks
- 2+ repeat postings from the same church (they found it useful enough to come back)
- At least one unsolicited referral ("my friend at X Church needs this")
- A completed transaction where both sides give positive feedback

### Yellow flags (adjust tactically):
- 2+ churches sign up but never post. *Fix: Personal follow-up within a week. "Can I help you draft your first need?"*
- Musicians start going quiet. *Fix: Transparent email: "Here's what we're doing to recruit churches. We need you to stay ready."*
- First 3 churches all want the same instrument (e.g., drummers only) and you don't have them. *Fix: Prioritize recruiting for that gap before the next church outreach.*

### Red flags (pivot needed):
- Zero churches posting after 6 weeks of outreach. *Pivot: Try one of the following — attend a Portland-area worship leaders gathering, host a coffee meetup for 3-4 worship leaders, or partner with one church deep as a pilot and use their case study to open doors.*
- First 3-4 completed transactions have friction (payment issues, communication problems, musicians not showing). *Pivot: Escort every booking personally until the process is smooth. Do not scale until the core experience works.*

## 10. What not to do

- **Don't run broad awareness ads.** "Hey Portland, check out Sunday Musician" with $200 gets you nothing. Use the budget for targeting worship leaders specifically.
- **Don't prioritize musician recruitment now.** You have supply. Build the demand side first. New musicians who join and see zero opportunities will churn.
- **Don't build new pages or features.** The app and site are ready. Every hour spent coding is an hour not getting a church to post a need.
- **Don't pitch "we have 15 musicians"** if those musicians don't cover what the target church needs. Be honest: "We have guitarists and vocalists. We're building the drummer list. Want to help shape who we recruit?"
- **Don't lead with the platform.** Lead with the problem and your personal experience serving at a Portland church. You and Jeramy are not "founders pitching a startup." You are "worship musicians who built something because we saw the gap."
